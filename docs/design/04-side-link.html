3Ranking losses

How to learn a relative ordering of items for a query, rather than an absolute score for each item.

3.1Pairwise loss

Optimizes preferences between pairs: for a query, the more relevant item should score above the less relevant one by some margin.

4Representation learning losses

How to shape an embedding space so that similar things land close together and dissimilar things land far apart.

4.1Contrastive learning

Pulls positive examples closer together in representation space and pushes negatives apart, so distance comes to mean dissimilarity.

How are they actually different?

ConnectsPairwise loss↔Contrastive learning
Notesboth ends inked

Pairwise ranking losses typically express that one candidate should score above another for a query, while contrastive objectives shape an embedding space by distinguishing positive pairs from negative candidates. They can overlap mathematically: the distinction depends on the learning setup, scoring function and negative sampling, not just the word “pairwise.”

Training-example setup

A typical ranking triplet has a query, a preferred and a less-preferred candidate. Contrastive training often compares one positive against many negatives at once.
